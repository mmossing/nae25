## Stage 118 – Granada → La Cruz (Tue 9 Dec 2025)

### Key places

**Granada historic center & Cathedral**  
Granada is one of the oldest European-founded cities on the mainland Americas and retains a compact colonial core with brightly painted facades, cobbled streets, and low-slung tile roofs. The yellow *Catedral de Nuestra Señora de la Asunción* on the central plaza is the visual anchor, with views over Lake Nicaragua and Volcán Mombacho from its tower.  [oai_citation:0‡Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada%2C_Nicaragua?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Lake Nicaragua waterfront & Isletas de Granada**  
From the east side of town a short spur leads to the lakefront and the small-boat docks for tours of the *Isletas de Granada*—hundreds of small volcanic islands formed by a prehistoric flank collapse of Mombacho. Tours typically leave from the Puerto de Asese docks at the end of the tourist center.  [oai_citation:1‡Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/isletas-de-granada?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Volcán Mombacho (landmark on your left as you ride south)**  
Mombacho is a 1,300-plus-meter stratovolcano just south of Granada, covered in cloud forest at higher elevations and designated as a nature reserve. Its historic debris avalanche created the Isletas archipelago and the Asese peninsula you skirt as you leave town.  [oai_citation:2‡Peakbagger](https://www.peakbagger.com/peak.aspx?pid=8146&utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Approach to La Cruz & Bahía Salinas**  
La Cruz sits on a ridge above Bahía Salinas near the Costa Rica–Nicaragua border. The town is small but has a central park and viewpoints over the bay and the Papagayo windswept coastline—your first clear sense of Costa Rica’s dry-forest Pacific.  [oai_citation:3‡Longitude Latitude Maps](https://www.longitude-latitude-maps.com/city/49_70%2CLa%2BCruz%2CGuanacaste%2CCosta%2BRica?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

### Stage character

- **Terrain:** Rolling out of Granada, then gradually climbing toward the border; more noticeable climbing near La Cruz.  
- **Climate:** Hot, often windy; you transition from the lake-influenced lowlands into the drier Guanacaste region. Expect strong sun and limited shade once you leave the foothills of Mombacho.  [oai_citation:4‡Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincia_de_Guanacaste?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  
- **Good photo / pause spots:**  
  - Plaza & cathedral in Granada (early morning light).  
  - Lakefront looking back toward the city and Mombacho.  
  - Late-day views from La Cruz toward Bahía Salinas.

Suggested “off-bike” option (evening before or early morning): short taxi ride to Puerto de Asese for a 1–2 h Isletas boat tour, if timing allows.

## Stage 119 – La Cruz → Playa del Coco (Wed 10 Dec 2025)

### Key places

**La Cruz ridge & views over Bahía Salinas**  
Leaving La Cruz you ride along ridges with expansive views north toward Nicaragua and west over Bahía Salinas. This is classic tropical dry forest—open pasture, scattered trees, big sky.  [oai_citation:5‡Longitude Latitude Maps](https://www.longitude-latitude-maps.com/city/49_70%2CLa%2BCruz%2CGuanacaste%2CCosta%2BRica?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Guanacaste dry forest landscapes**  
Much of the day runs through Guanacaste’s interior: cattle country, seasonal rivers, and stretches of forest adapted to a long dry season from roughly November to May. This region’s ecology and land use underpin Costa Rica’s “sabanas” grazing economy and its tourism boom along the north Pacific coast.  [oai_citation:6‡Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincia_de_Guanacaste?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Playa Hermosa (Guanacaste) – optional side trip**  
Just north of Playas del Coco lies Playa Hermosa, a wide crescent of light sand, calm water, and low green hills. It has “Bandera Azul” environmental certification and is marketed as a quieter alternative to Coco.  [oai_citation:7‡Mapcarta](https://mapcarta.com/W113048698?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Playas del Coco beachfront (stage finish)**  
Playas del Coco is one of Costa Rica’s oldest beach towns, a hub for diving and fishing trips to the Gulf of Papagayo and offshore islands. The main beach is a long, gently curving bay with a palm-lined promenade and sunset views west over the Pacific.  [oai_citation:8‡latlong.net](https://www.latlong.net/place/playa-del-coco-beach-playas-del-coco-costa-rica-23765.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

### Stage character

- **Terrain:** Mostly rolling, with modest climbs as you move away from the border ridge and then back toward the coast.  
- **Heat management:** This is prime “oven” territory—plan for high temperatures and relatively low humidity compared to the Caribbean or central highlands. Mid-stage resupply in roadside “sodas” is key.  [oai_citation:9‡Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincia_de_Guanacaste?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  
- **Evening options in Coco:**  
  - Beach walk or swim at sunset.  
  - Boat-based bioluminescence or night-snorkel tours (weather and tour availability dependent).

## Stage 120 – Playa del Coco → Puntarenas (Thu 11 Dec 2025)

### Key places

**Papagayo / Nicoya coastal corridor (en route)**  
From Coco you work your way southeast through Guanacaste toward the Gulf of Nicoya. The landscape gradually shifts from the drier northern savannas toward more humid lowlands, with roadside teak and other plantation forestry mixed with cattle and small farms.  [oai_citation:10‡Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincia_de_Guanacaste?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Historic port city of Puntarenas (stage finish)**  
Puntarenas occupies a narrow sand spit extending into the Gulf of Nicoya. Historically it was Costa Rica’s principal Pacific port before shipping shifted to Caldera; today it’s a mix of working port, ferry hub, and low-key seaside resort. The *Paseo de los Turistas* waterfront promenade runs along the outer shore with food stalls, ice-cream stands, and views across the gulf to the Nicoya Peninsula.  [oai_citation:11‡Waze](https://www.waze.com/es-419/live-map/directions/paseo-de-los-turistas%2C-puntarenas-paseo-de-los-turistas-av.-4-puntarenas-centro%2C-puntarenas?to=place.w.180355172.1803355110.8783591&utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Mangroves and coastal wetlands near Puntarenas**  
The estuaries and mangroves around Puntarenas are important nurseries for fish and bird life; several local operators offer short boat tours into mangrove channels, often marketed as crocodile and bird-watching trips.  [oai_citation:12‡Visit Costa Rica](https://www.visitcostarica.com/where-to-go/protected-areas/carara-national-park?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

### Stage character

- **Terrain:** Long but not extreme—steady rollers and some longer low-gradient climbs and descents.  
- **Visual arc:** You effectively ride from one tourist coast (Papagayo) to another (central Pacific), tracing the spine of Costa Rica’s Pacific lowlands.  

Evening in Puntarenas is well-suited to a recovery walk along the Paseo, people-watching, and re-fueling with something salty and cold.

## Stage 121 – Puntarenas → Quepos (Fri 12 Dec 2025)

This is one of the most scenic—and biologically rich—days of the whole tour, as you traverse the transition between dry forest and true Pacific rainforest.

### Key places

**Tarcoles River “Crocodile Bridge” (Route 34)**  
South of Orotina the route crosses the Río Grande de Tárcoles on a highway bridge famous for the dense population of large American crocodiles visible from the pedestrian walkway. This “Crocodile Bridge” has become a classic roadside stop with informal viewing platforms and souvenir stands.  [oai_citation:13‡MytanFeet](https://mytanfeet.com/activities/rio-tarcoles-crocodiles-costa-rica/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Carara National Park (transition forest hotspot)**  
Roughly 2 km south of the Tarcoles bridge lies the main entrance to Carara National Park, protecting ~52 km² of forest at the biological hinge between the dry north-Pacific and the wetter central-Pacific zones. The park is renowned for its scarlet macaws, diverse birdlife, and a forest structure that changes noticeably over just a few kilometers of trail.  [oai_citation:14‡Latitude.to](https://latitude.to/articles-by-country/cr/costa-rica/74668/carara-national-park?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

Short, flat trails near the entrance make this one of the more accessible rain-forest walks in Costa Rica—an option for riders who arrive with time and energy to spare or for a rest-day shuttle back from Quepos.

**Central Pacific surf beaches (Hermosa, Esterillos)**  
Continuing south along Route 34 you pass access roads to Playa Hermosa and the Playa Hermosa–Punta Mala Wildlife Refuge, key nesting habitat for olive ridley sea turtles and a well-known surf break, as well as the long gray-sand beaches of Esterillos.  [oai_citation:15‡Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_Hermosa-Punta_Mala_Wildlife_Refuge?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

**Arrival in Quepos**  
Quepos sits on a small bay with a backdrop of steep, forested hills leading toward Manuel Antonio. It began as a banana and oil-palm export port and is now strongly oriented toward tourism, especially game fishing and visits to Manuel Antonio National Park.  [oai_citation:16‡Vianica.com](https://vianica.com/activity/29/boat-trip-among-the-granada-islets?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

The modern *Marina Pez Vela* on the waterfront is a large sport-fishing and yachting facility with restaurants, shops, and a broad promenade—an easy place for an evening stroll.  [oai_citation:17‡Mapcarta](https://mapcarta.com/W150323592?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

### Stage character

- **Terrain:** Rolling to hilly, with cumulative climbing as you follow the coastal foothills; more humid and often hotter than earlier stages.  
- **Natural history arc:** You move from former mangrove and floodplain near Puntarenas through dry-to-wet transition forest (Carara) into lush lowland rainforest near Quepos and Manuel Antonio.  [oai_citation:18‡Visit Costa Rica](https://www.visitcostarica.com/where-to-go/protected-areas/carara-national-park?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Rest Day – Quepos (Sat 13 Dec 2025)

### Manuel Antonio National Park

The obvious focal point for your rest day is Manuel Antonio National Park, a small but extraordinarily biodiverse coastal park just south of Quepos and one of Costa Rica’s most visited protected areas. Despite its size, the park combines very humid tropical forest, mangroves, rocky headlands, islands, and some of the country’s most photogenic beaches.  [oai_citation:19‡Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Antonio_National_Park?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

The trail network is short but rich in wildlife: white-faced capuchins, howler and squirrel monkeys, sloths, agoutis, coatis, and a great variety of birds and reptiles are commonly seen along the main paths and near the beaches. Beaches such as Manuel Antonio and Espadilla Sur offer sheltered swimming and tide-pooling.  [oai_citation:20‡Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Antonio_National_Park?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

Practical notes:

- Park tickets must be reserved online in advance and are limited per time slot.  
- The park is closed on Tuesdays but otherwise open daily; your rest day is a Saturday so it will be busy—an early start helps.  [oai_citation:21‡National Park Manuel Antonio](https://nationalparkmanuelantonio.com/manuel-antonio-national-park/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

### Quepos & Marina Pez Vela

Quepos itself rewards some wandering: the older town grid just behind the waterfront, local bakeries and sodas, and the more polished environment of Marina Pez Vela with its restaurants, ice-cream, and sunset views over moored boats.  [oai_citation:22‡Vianica.com](https://vianica.com/activity/29/boat-trip-among-the-granada-islets?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

### Beaches outside the park

If you prefer to skip park logistics (or go a second day), the public *Playa Espadilla Norte* north of the park boundary offers a long, accessible surf- and swim-friendly beach with sunset views and beach-chair rentals, without needing park entry.  [oai_citation:23‡Costa Rica Info Link](https://costaricainfolink.com/en/espadilla-norte-beach-costa-rica/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Regional cultural & historical context

- **Granada & Lake Nicaragua:** Granada was founded in 1524 and became an early Spanish administrative and commercial center, linked to the Caribbean via the Río San Juan. Its surviving colonial architecture reflects both its strategic importance and repeated episodes of conflict and fire. The nearby Mombacho volcano and Isletas archipelago illustrate the tight coupling of human settlement with volcanic and lacustrine landscapes in Central America.  [oai_citation:24‡Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada%2C_Nicaragua?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

- **Guanacaste & the north Pacific dry forest:** Crossing into Costa Rica, the route traverses Guanacaste Province, historically dominated by cattle ranching and grain agriculture, now also by beach tourism along Papagayo and the Nicoya Peninsula. A pronounced dry season shapes both the natural vegetation and land use—classic deciduous “bosque tropical seco” with scattered large trees over pasture.  [oai_citation:25‡Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincia_de_Guanacaste?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

- **Central Pacific transition zone:** Around Tarcoles and Carara National Park you pass through one of Costa Rica’s best examples of transition forest, where northern dry-forest species overlap with wetter southern coastal assemblages. Carara’s scarlet macaws are emblematic of national conservation efforts to recover species once extirpated from much of the Pacific lowlands.  [oai_citation:26‡Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carara_National_Park?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

- **Quepos, Manuel Antonio & tourism:** Quepos evolved from banana plantations and smallholder agriculture to a tourism-centered economy anchored by Manuel Antonio National Park and sport-fishing. The park itself was created after local pressure resisted private coastal development and beach privatization, making it a case study in how community activism can shape conservation policy.  [oai_citation:27‡Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Antonio_National_Park?utm_source=chatgpt.com)  

This 4-day segment thus links a colonial lakeside city, the pastoral dry-forest lowlands of Guanacaste, and the humid Pacific rainforests of the central coast—an unusually compressed cross-section of Central American environments and histories.

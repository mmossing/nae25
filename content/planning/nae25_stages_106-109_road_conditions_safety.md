## Stage-by-Stage Road Conditions

### Stage 106: Tapachula → Coatepeque (82 km)
**Date:** November 24, 2025
**Border Crossing Day**

#### Road Surface
- **Mexican side (Tapachula to border):** Paved, good condition
- **Border zone:** Multiple crossing options available
- **Guatemalan side:** CA-1 highway begins, paved 2-4 lanes with shoulder

#### Border Crossing Information

**Recommended Crossing: El Carmen/Talisman**
- **Location:** 41 km from Tapachula
- **Hours:** 24/7 operation
- **Process Time:** 30-60 minutes typical
- **Facilities:** Money changers, small shops, basic services

**Alternative: Ciudad Hidalgo/Tecún Umán**
- More crowded, primarily commercial traffic
- Can be confusing for first-time crossers
- Not recommended for cyclists due to heavy truck traffic

**Border Crossing Procedures:**

1. **Exit Mexico:**
   - Present passport at Mexican immigration (may need to search for office)
   - If in Mexico >7 days: May owe exit tax (~400 MXN/$20 USD)
   - Obtain exit stamp
   - Small bridge toll (Mexican pesos or Guatemalan quetzals accepted)

2. **Enter Guatemala:**
   - Complete electronic immigration form (can do online in advance): [Guatemala Immigration Portal](https://declaraguate.sat.gob.gt)
   - Present passport at Guatemalan immigration
   - Receive entry stamp (90-day stay typically granted)
   - No entry fees for tourists
   - Currency declaration if carrying >$10,000 USD

**Post-Border Checkpoints:**
- Expect 2-4 police/military checkpoints between border and Coatepeque
- Have passport ready for inspection
- Quick passport checks, rarely detain cyclists
- Be polite, smile, have documents organized

#### Traffic Conditions
- **Morning (6-10 AM):** Moderate traffic, heavy trucks beginning routes
- **Midday (10 AM-3 PM):** Heaviest traffic period
- **Afternoon (3-6 PM):** Moderate, improving
- **Weekends:** Generally lighter traffic

#### Safety Notes
- Border area has UK Foreign Office advisory (within 5km of border)
- Stick to main CA-1 highway
- Stay in groups through border zone
- Don't accept help from unauthorized "guides" or money changers
- Use official immigration offices only

### Stage 107: Coatepeque → Quetzaltenango (63 km)
**Date:** November 25, 2025
**⚠️ Exceptionally Challenging Stage**

#### Road Surface
- **CA-1 Highway:** Paved, 2-4 lanes with shoulder
- **Condition:** Generally good pavement, some potholes
- **Gradient:** 2,493m elevation gain - 40m/km average!

#### Construction Updates
- No major construction reported on this segment (November 2025)
- CA-1 tolls: $2-5 USD per section (cash or card)
- PROVIAL (roadside assistance) patrols this highway

#### Traffic Hazards
- **Heavy commercial traffic:** Large trucks, especially morning hours
- **Passing vehicles on climbs:** Motorcycles weave between lanes
- **Limited shoulders:** Varies by section, not always present
- **Aggressive driving:** Common throughout Guatemala
- **Horn use:** Constant, don't take it personally

#### Safety Considerations
- **This is a MAJOR CLIMBING DAY:** Take breaks, hydrate constantly
- Altitude increases from 622m to 2,340m
- Temperature drops significantly with elevation
- Morning temps in Quetzaltenango can be 5-14°C in November
- Fog possible in upper elevations

#### Checkpoint Locations
- Highway checkpoint approximately Km 188 (San Cristóbal, Totonicapán area)
- Possible weigh stations for trucks (cyclists wave through)
- Police presence increases near Quetzaltenango

### Stage 108: Quetzaltenango → Santiago Atitlán (103 km)
**Date:** November 26, 2025

#### Route Options

**Primary Route (CA-1 to Panajachel, then boat):**
- Well-paved CA-1 highway
- Heavy traffic, 2-4 lanes
- Most direct but least scenic
- Boat (lancha) from Panajachel to Santiago Atitlán

**Alternative Route (via Patzún):**
- More isolated, lower traffic
- Reports of past holdups in canyon sections
- Stunning scenery but vulnerable on climbs
- **Not recommended for solo cyclists**
- Consider ASISTUR security escort if using this route

#### Road Surface
- **CA-1 segments:** Good pavement, wide shoulders
- **Secondary roads to lake:** Variable quality, narrow in places
- **Panajachel area:** Well-traveled, tourist traffic

#### Lake Atitlán Access
- **Boat services:** Regular lanchas between all lakeside towns
- **Operating hours:** Daylight hours primarily (last boats ~6 PM)
- **Cost:** Fixed rates, posted at docks
- **Safety:** Boats are safe and well-maintained
- **Bikes:** Easily accommodated on lanchas

#### Specific Safety Concerns
- **Godinez-Patzún route:** Past reports of armed robberies in isolated canyon sections
- **Irish Foreign Office advisory:** Avoid Godinez bypass via Patzún; use Pan-American (CA-1) to Sololá instead
- **Recommendation:** Stick to CA-1 main highway for this stage

- **Thursday before Thanksgiving:** May have some tourist traffic to Antigua

#### Safety Notes - Antigua Area
- **Cerro de la Cruz:** Avoid hiking early morning or late afternoon (robbery reports)
- **Within Antigua:** Very safe, visible tourist police (DISETUR)
- **Nighttime:** Stay in central areas, use hotel-arranged taxis
- **Zona 1:** Historic center - safe and well-patrolled

## General Road Safety Information

### Road Conditions Throughout Guatemala

#### Primary Highways (CA-1, CA-9)
- Generally paved and well-maintained
- Tolls on major segments ($2-5 per section)
- Heavy commercial traffic, especially daytime
- 2-4 lanes with variable shoulder width
- Speed limits: 90 km/h highways, 80 km/h rural, 50 km/h urban
- **Reality:** Speed limits frequently ignored

#### Secondary Roads
- Quality varies significantly
- Many unpaved in highland areas
- Rain can create mudslides, rockslides (even in dry season)
- Fog common in highland mornings
- Little to no nighttime illumination

#### Known Hazards
- **Potholes:** Common, even on main highways
- **Unmarked road hazards:** Speed bumps, construction, obstacles
- **Landslides:** Possible after any rain, even November
- **Pedestrians:** Often walk on roads, limited crosswalks
- **Livestock:** Occasional on rural roads
- **Turn signals:** Rarely used by drivers
- **Passing:** Blind passing on curves common
- **One-way streets:** Not always observed

### Driving Culture
- **Rules are suggestions:** Stop signs, lane markings often ignored
- **Hand signals:** Common - driver waves hand out window to indicate unspecified action
- **Horn use:** Constant, not considered rude
- **Right of way:** Larger vehicle usually assumes priority
- **Drinking and driving:** Common, especially weekends/evenings

## Police & Military Checkpoints

### Checkpoint Types

#### 1. Immigration Checkpoints (INM)
- **Frequency:** 2-12 along routes from border
- **Purpose:** Check legal status in country
- **What to expect:** Passport inspection, may ask destination
- **For cyclists:** Usually quick, rarely detained
- **Documents needed:** Passport with valid entry stamp

#### 2. Military/Police Highway Checkpoints
- **Purpose:** Drug interdiction, contraband, vehicle inspection
- **Staffed by:** National Police (PNC), Military Police, Mexican Army, Navy
- **Frequency:** Variable, 12+ strategic locations nationwide
- **Process:** Vehicle stop, possible bag inspection
- **For cyclists:** Often waved through, may ask quick questions

#### 3. Weigh Stations
- **Purpose:** Commercial truck weighing
- **For cyclists:** No impact, continue past

#### 4. PROVIAL Assistance Points
- **Purpose:** Roadside assistance, not enforcement
- **Contact:** +502 2422-7800 or +502 2419-2121
- **Services:** Basic tools, first aid, free assistance
- **Locations:** Major highways including CA-1

### Checkpoint Best Practices

**Documents to Carry:**
- Passport (original) with entry stamp
- Copy of passport photo page (backup)
- Hotel reservation confirmation
- Emergency contact information
- Travel insurance information

**Checkpoint Behavior:**
- Remain calm and polite
- Have passport easily accessible
- Smile, greet officers respectfully
- Answer questions briefly and honestly
- "Turista, en bicicleta" (tourist, on bicycle) usually sufficient
- Don't volunteer extra information
- Don't photograph checkpoints
- Don't argue or show frustration

**Potential Issues:**
- **Corrupt officials:** May request "fines" or "tolls"
- **Strategy:** Politely play dumb, say "No entiendo" (I don't understand)
- **Escalation:** Ask to speak to superior officer
- **Payment:** Legitimate tolls are clearly marked; checkpoint "fines" are usually not legitimate
- **If pressured:** Small payment (20-50 quetzals) may resolve situation

#### Low Risk (Unlikely to affect cyclists)
- Gang violence (internal conflicts, not tourist-focused)

- Night-time travel outside cities

## Health & Medical Considerations

### Altitude Concerns
Your stages involve significant altitude changes:
- Tapachula: 116m
- Coatepeque: 622m
- **Quetzaltenango: 2,340m** ← Significant altitude
- Lake Atitlán: 1,562m
- Antigua: 1,532m

**Stage 107 Altitude Gain:** 1,718m in 63 km - monitor for altitude sickness symptoms.

**Symptoms to Watch:**
- Headache
- Nausea
- Dizziness
- Shortness of breath beyond normal exertion
- Fatigue

**Prevention:**
- Gradual ascent (you're doing this - riding up)
- Hydrate constantly
- Avoid alcohol in Quetzaltenango
- Consider acetazolamide (Diamox) if prone to altitude sickness
- Sleep lower if possible (Antigua lower than Quetzaltenango)

### Medical Facilities

**Quetzaltenango:**
- Hospital Nacional (public)
- Hospital Privado Quetzaltenango (private, better quality)

**Antigua:**
- Hospital Nacional Pedro de Bethancourt (public)
- Several private clinics
- English-speaking doctors available

**Guatemala City (if needed):**
- Private hospitals comparable to U.S./European standards
- Expensive without insurance
- 1-1.5 hours from Antigua

### Travel Insurance
- **CRITICAL:** Ensure coverage includes:
  - Medical evacuation
  - Cycling/adventure sports
  - Guatemala specifically
  - Emergency medical
  - Theft/gear replacement

### Water Safety
- **Do NOT drink tap water** anywhere
- Use bottled water only (widely available)
- Avoid ice unless you know source
- Brush teeth with bottled water
- Restaurants: Bottled water usually provided

### Mosquito-Borne Illness
- **November:** Lower mosquito season (dry season)
- **Dengue:** Present but lower risk in highlands
- **Zika:** Minimal risk
- **Malaria:** Not typically issue in highland routes
- **Prevention:** Repellent for lowland areas (Tapachula, Coatepeque)

## During-Ride Safety Tips

### Group Riding
- Stay together, especially in border area
- Support riders on difficult climbs (Stage 107)
- Communicate hazards to riders behind
- Designated sweep rider
- Regular regroup points

### Solo Riding (if applicable)
- Inform others of daily route and ETA
- Check in at hotels/rest stops
- Carry fully charged phone
- Consider ASISTUR escort for isolated sections
- Join other cyclists when possible

### Interactions with Locals
- Be friendly and respectful
- Learn basic Spanish phrases
- Wave/acknowledge greetings
- Support local businesses
- Don't display wealth ostentatiously
- Respect cultural norms (modest clothing in small towns)

### If Problems Arise

**Mechanical Issue:**
- Pull completely off road
- Use hazard lights if you have them
- Group protects mechanic from traffic
- PROVIAL can assist on major highways

**Accident:**
- Stop, assess injuries
- Call 110 (police) and 122 (ambulance) if needed
- ASISTUR 1500 for tourist-specific assistance
- Do not leave scene until police arrive (legal requirement)
- Document everything if possible
- Contact tour support and insurance

**Confrontation/Robbery:**
- Comply with demands - possessions replaceable, you're not
- Give up wallet, phone, camera if demanded
- Do not resist or chase
- Note descriptions of people, vehicles
- Report to ASISTUR 1500 and police 110 immediately
- Contact embassy if passport stolen
- File police report for insurance claims

**Lost/Separated:**
- Stay on main road
- Return to last known meeting point
- Contact tour support/group members
- Don't venture on side roads searching
- Wait at safe public location (gas station, restaurant)

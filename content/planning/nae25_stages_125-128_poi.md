## Stage 125: Volcán to Playa Las Lajas
**Thursday, December 18, 2025**
**147 km | +859m / -2,216m | Epic Descent Day**

### Stage Highlights

This stage begins in the cool Chiriquí Highlands and descends dramatically over 2,200 meters to the Pacific coast. The first 35 km feature an extended downhill with good road shoulders, transitioning from cloud forest to tropical lowlands. Special note: This is **Jairo's 40th Birthday** celebration!

### Points of Interest Near Volcán (Start)

**Volcán Barú National Park**
Panama's highest peak at 3,475 meters (11,401 feet), this dormant stratovolcano is the only place in the world where, on clear days, you can see both the Pacific and Caribbean oceans simultaneously. The surrounding cloud forests support remarkable biodiversity including the elusive Resplendent Quetzal.

**Sitio Barriles Archaeological Site**
Pre-Columbian ruins dating from 300-900 AD, located 6 km west of Volcán along the Río Gariche. Statues discovered here depict figures in conical hats riding on the shoulders of others, suggesting hierarchical social structures. The Barriles people may have inhabited this area as early as 734 BC.

**Lagunas de Volcán**
Panama's highest lake system at 1,240 meters elevation, these two protected wetland lakes offer excellent birdwatching opportunities. Species include pale-billed woodpeckers, flycatchers, antbirds, and masked ducks. Located 4 km west of Volcán.

**Parque Internacional La Amistad (PILA)**
UNESCO World Heritage Site spanning the Panama-Costa Rica border, this park protects one of Central America's largest remaining tracts of virgin cloud forest. Home to nearly 600 bird species and diverse wildlife including jaguars and tapirs.

### Route Corridor Attractions

**Coffee Plantations**
The Chiriquí Highlands produce world-renowned Geisha coffee, grown in the volcanic soil at high altitudes. This is one of the most expensive coffee varieties globally. Watch for roadside coffee farms during the descent.

**Los Cangilones de Gualaca**
Natural rock formations with crystal-clear swimming pools, located in the transition zone between highlands and lowlands. A refreshing stop if taking a scenic detour.

### Destination: Playa Las Lajas

**Playa Las Lajas**
One of Panama's longest beaches stretching 15 km along the Pacific coast in the Gulf of Chiriquí. The wide, gray-sand beach offers gentle waves perfect for swimming, with warm water temperatures year-round. Sea turtles (Olive Ridley) nest here from July-September. Spectacular sunsets are a daily highlight.

**Gulf of Chiriquí Marine Park**
The warm, protected waters of this marine park serve as birthing grounds for Humpback Whales (best viewing July-September). The park includes coral reefs, mangroves, and diverse marine ecosystems with opportunities for snorkeling and island hopping.

### Stage Notes

- Extended 35 km downhill section with good road shoulders
- Temperature transition from cool highlands (~20°C) to hot coastal lowlands (~32°C)
- Pack layers for the morning start, plan to shed clothing during descent
- Sunscreen essential for the coastal finish

### Accommodation
**Hotel Las Lajas Beach Resort** - Beachfront hotel with pool, restaurant, and ocean views

## Stage 126: Playa Las Lajas to Santiago
**Friday, December 19, 2025**
**133 km | +1,778m / -1,701m | Party Ride!**

### Stage Highlights

**PARTY RIDE DAY!** This stage celebrates human-powered adventure, friendship, and a successful expedition. Decorate your bike, wear your favorite Hawaiian or party shirt, add some glitter—whatever brings you joy! Shops in town have party ride accessories.

The route follows the Pan-American Highway through agricultural lowlands of Chiriquí and into Veraguas province, crossing the provincial boundary at the Río Tabasará.

### Route Corridor Attractions

**San Félix**
Small town with access to indigenous Ngäbe-Buglé communities in the surrounding mountains. The area features traditional indigenous trails and opportunities for cultural encounters with Panama's largest indigenous group.

**Nancito Petroglyphs**
Archaeological site featuring ancient rock carvings, with nearby Cascada Escondida (Hidden Waterfall) emerging from lush vegetation.

**David City** (If detoured or passed through)
Capital of Chiriquí Province and Panama's second-largest city. The Parque de Cervantes serves as the cultural heart of the city with regular events and local markets.

### Destination: Santiago de Veraguas

**Santiago de Veraguas**
Capital of Veraguas Province and one of Panama's largest cities, Santiago serves as the main transportation hub of central Panama. Located midway between David and Panama City on the Pan-American Highway.

**Catedral Santiago Apóstol**
Colonial-era cathedral on the west end of town containing the remains of General José de Fábrega, a key figure in Panama's independence from Spain in 1821.

**La Escuela Normal Superior Juan Demóstenes Arosemena**
Teachers' college featuring distinctive architectural designs including large columns supporting an archway with notable statues. Inside, the theatre contains murals by famous Panamanian artist Roberto Lewis.

**Museo Regional de Veraguas**
Housed in a former 1855 prison building, this museum displays over 2,000 years of regional history. Funded by the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute, exhibits include archaeological artifacts, pre-Columbian ceramics, and ecological displays.

**Centro Interactivo Canal de Panamá**
Interactive museum in Santiago showcasing Panama Canal history and the nation's historical significance.

**Parque Juan Demóstenes**
Central park featuring the monument to Doctor Juan Bautista Amador García, hero of Panama's separation from Colombia and first governor of Veraguas.

### Regional Context

Veraguas is the only province in Panama (and one of few in the world) with coastlines on both the Atlantic and Pacific oceans. The province offers diverse landscapes from Pacific beaches to Caribbean rainforests.

**Nearby Attractions (Day Trip Potential):**
- **Santa Catalina** - World-class surfing destination and gateway to Coiba Island
- **Parque Nacional Coiba** - UNESCO World Heritage Site, former prison island, spectacular diving
- **Santa Fe** - Mountain town famous for orchids, coffee, and waterfalls (57 km from Santiago)

### Stage Notes

- Party Ride theme: Decorate bikes and wear festive attire
- Hot, humid conditions throughout
- Services available in towns along Pan-American Highway
- Stock up on party supplies in Santiago for the celebration

### Accommodation
**Hotel Mykonos** - Full-service hotel on the Pan-American Highway across from Santiago Airport, featuring pool, restaurant, and bar

## Stage 127: Santiago to Penonomé
**Saturday, December 20, 2025**
**95 km | +405m / -410m | Pan-American Highway**

### Stage Highlights

The shortest stage of this final block follows the Pan-American Highway through the heartland of Panama. Expect **busy traffic all day** as this is the main artery connecting Panama City to western Panama. The route passes through agricultural regions famous for sugarcane, rice, and citrus production.

### Route Corridor Attractions

**Iglesia de San Francisco de la Montaña**
Historic baroque church north of Santiago, beautifully maintained since 1630 and declared a national treasure. Features original colonial architecture and religious art.

**Reserva La Yeguada**
Protected reserve featuring picturesque pine forests and a crystal-clear blue lake formed by an ancient volcanic crater. Popular camping destination with clean, maintained grounds.

**Natá de los Caballeros**
One of the oldest continuously inhabited towns in the Americas, founded in 1522. Home to the **Basilica Menor Santiago Apóstol**, thought to be the oldest church in the Americas still in use today. Indigenous artisans contributed to its construction.

**El Caño Archaeological Park**
Important pre-Columbian site discovered in 1925, containing burial mounds dating from AD 500-1200. The 2011 discovery of a warrior chief's tomb revealed significant artifacts. Located a short drive from Penonomé.

### Destination: Penonomé

**Penonomé**
Capital of Coclé Province, Penonomé is a bustling Panamanian town with distinct Spanish-colonial charm in its downtown area. Located 143 km west of Panama City, it served briefly as Panama's capital when Panama City was under pirate threat.

**Catedral de Penonomé**
Well-preserved cathedral with colorful stained glass windows, facing the pleasant town plaza with its gazebo and benches—perfect for experiencing local Panamanian life.

**Museo de Penonomé**
Small but thorough museum in a beautiful blue-and-white colonial building, examining the history and traditions of Coclé Province with collections of pre-Columbian pottery, religious art, and traditional furniture.

**La Pintada**
Small foothill town 12 km northwest of Penonomé, famous for its artisans' market specializing in **sombreros pintados** ("painted hats"), the distinctive woven hats unique to Panama. Also features a cigar factory with internationally recognized hand-rolled Cuban-seed puros.

**Penonome Market**
Vibrant public market with fresh produce, local snacks, and traditional Panamanian food stalls. Great place for authentic local cuisine and people-watching.

### Regional Context

Coclé Province is one of Panama's most visited regions thanks to proximity to Panama City and diverse scenery. The Pacific Riviera beaches (Playa Santa Clara, Playa Blanca, Farallón) lie southwest of Penonomé. The province also encompasses El Valle de Antón, a town nestled in an extinct volcanic crater.

### Stage Notes

- **Heavy traffic expected** - Pan-American Highway carries significant commercial and private vehicle traffic
- Stay alert and maintain single-file formation
- Take breaks in towns to escape highway conditions
- Consider early start to minimize peak traffic exposure

### Accommodation
**Hotel Coclé** - 110-room hotel at Iguana Mall on the Via Interamericana, featuring pool, restaurant, and fitness center

## Stage 128: Penonomé to Panama City
**Sunday, December 21, 2025**
**102 km | +610m / -683m | Final Stage - Ferry Finish!**

### Stage Highlights

**THE FINAL STAGE!** This day concludes the North American Epic with a unique ferry crossing to Panama City. The route follows the Pan-American Highway before turning toward Punta Chame, where cyclists board a ferry across the Bay of Panama to the Amador Causeway, arriving at the historic canal entrance.

**Final Dinner at 7:00 PM** - Celebration of completing the journey from the Arctic to Panama!

### Route Corridor Attractions

**Pacific Riviera Beaches** (visible from highway)
The route passes turnoffs to popular beach destinations including Playa Coronado, Gorgona, and Playa Santa Clara—all within 1-2 hours of Panama City.

**Punta Chame Peninsula**
A narrow finger of land jutting into the Pacific Ocean, popular for kitesurfing and windsurfing due to consistent strong winds. The peninsula offers views of the Bay of Chame, Taborcillo Island, and the distant Panama City skyline. Sea turtle conservation projects operate on these beaches.

### Ferry Experience: Punta Chame to Panama City

The tour finishes with a memorable **ferry crossing** across the Bay of Panama, offering stunning views of:
- The Pacific entrance to the Panama Canal
- Bridge of the Americas (completed 1962)
- Panama City's modern skyline
- Ships waiting to transit the canal

The ferry arrives at or near the **Amador Causeway**, a 6 km scenic road built 1904-1914 using rock excavated during canal construction. The causeway connects four islands (Naos, Culebra, Perico, and Flamenco) to the mainland.

### Destination: Panama City

**Panama Canal**
One of the modern wonders of the world, this 80 km waterway connects the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans, fundamentally changing global shipping. Over 30 ships transit daily. The **Miraflores Visitor Center** offers observation decks to watch massive ships navigate the locks.

**Casco Viejo (Casco Antiguo)**
UNESCO World Heritage Site, this charming historic district features narrow cobblestone streets, colonial architecture, and colorful buildings with wrought-iron balconies. Must-see sites include:
- **Plaza de la Independencia** - Main square with the Presidential Palace
- **Iglesia de San José** - Home to the legendary **Altar de Oro** (Golden Altar), painted black to hide it from pirate Henry Morgan in 1671
- **Plaza de Francia** - Monument to the 22,000 workers who died building the canal, featuring the French Embassy and beautiful bay views
- **Arco Chato** - Famous flat arch that survived centuries of earthquakes
- **Paseo Esteban Huertas** - Bougainvillea-covered waterfront promenade

**Panama Canal Museum**
Located in a handsome historic building, this museum documents the canal's history through photographs, artifacts, and interactive displays—essential for understanding Panama's significance to world trade.

**Biomuseo**
Frank Gehry's first design for Latin America, this dramatically colorful building on the Amador Causeway explores Panama's biodiversity and the ecological impact of the land bridge formation.

**Cerro Ancón (Ancón Hill)**
Highest point in Panama City, offering panoramic views of both Casco Viejo and the modern city. Accessible via a 30-minute hike through urban rainforest.

**Panama Viejo**
UNESCO World Heritage Site containing ruins of the original Panama City, destroyed by pirate Henry Morgan in 1671. The first European settlement on the Pacific coast of the Americas.

**Cinta Costera**
Spectacular 9 km waterfront promenade stretching along Panama Bay, offering views of the modern skyline, green spaces, and the Pacific Ocean.

### Stage Notes

- Final stage—pace yourself for the celebration!
- Ferry logistics will be coordinated by tour staff
- Have passport accessible for any documentation needs
- Arrive camera-ready for memorable finish photos
- Final Dinner at 7:00 PM at hotel

### Accommodation
**Radisson Hotel Panama Canal** - Located on the Amador Causeway at the mouth of the Panama Canal, featuring views of the Bridge of the Americas, outdoor pool, and multiple restaurants

## Practical Information

### Weather Expectations (December)

December marks the transition into Panama's dry season, with generally favorable cycling conditions:
- **Temperatures:** 28-33°C (82-91°F) during the day
- **Humidity:** 70-85%
- **Rain:** Decreasing, occasional afternoon showers possible
- **Conditions:** Hot and humid, especially on coastal stages

### Currency

Panama uses the **US Dollar (USD)** as official currency (alongside the Balboa, which is pegged 1:1). Credit cards widely accepted in cities; carry cash for small towns and markets.

### Language

Spanish is the official language. English is spoken in tourist areas and hotels but less common in rural areas.

### Safety Considerations

- **Traffic:** Pan-American Highway carries heavy truck and bus traffic; maintain vigilance
- **Hydration:** Essential in tropical heat; drink before you're thirsty
- **Sun protection:** Strong equatorial sun; use SPF 50+ and reapply frequently
- **Road shoulders:** Variable quality; be prepared for narrow sections

### Cultural Notes

**Panamanian Food Highlights:**
- **Sancocho** - Traditional chicken soup, Panama's national dish
- **Ceviche** - Fresh seafood marinated in citrus
- **Patacones** - Fried green plantains
- **Arroz con pollo** - Chicken with rice
- **Raspao** - Shaved ice with fruit syrup and condensed milk

**Sombreros Pintados:**
Panama's famous woven hats (not to be confused with "Panama hats" from Ecuador) are hand-woven from natural fibers. The Penonomé/La Pintada region is the traditional center of this craft.

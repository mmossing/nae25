## Rest Day: Quepos (Saturday, December 13, 2025)

Quepos serves as the gateway to one of Costa Rica's most beloved national parks. Take advantage of this rest day to explore the rich biodiversity of Manuel Antonio or simply relax on world-class beaches.

### Manuel Antonio National Park
**GPS:** 9.3925, -84.1367

Costa Rica's smallest yet most visited national park, Manuel Antonio combines stunning beaches with exceptional wildlife viewing. Established in 1972, the park protects 683 hectares of tropical rainforest, pristine beaches, and coral reefs.

**Wildlife highlights:**
- White-faced capuchin monkeys (commonly seen)
- Two-toed and three-toed sloths
- Central American squirrel monkey (endemic subspecies - only 1,500 individuals remain)
- Howler monkeys
- 350+ bird species including toucans and trogons

**Key attractions within the park:**
- Playa Manuel Antonio - rated among the world's top 25 beaches
- Playa Espadilla Sur - less crowded, excellent for swimming
- Punta Catedral - former island connected by tombolo, with excellent viewpoints
- Pre-Columbian Quepoas turtle traps (visible at low tide at western end of Playa Manuel Antonio)
- 14-hectare lagoon and mangroves

**Practical information:**
- Hours: Wednesday-Monday, 7:00 AM - 3:00 PM (Closed Tuesdays)
- Entry: $18 USD for non-residents (online purchase required via sinac.go.cr)
- Daily visitor limit: ~1,200 people
- No food allowed inside (small cafe available)
- Guided tours highly recommended for wildlife spotting
- Contact: +506 2777-4221

**Tips for cyclists:**
- Book tickets online in advance as they sell out
- Arrive early (7 AM opening) for cooler temperatures and more active wildlife
- Bring water only - food prohibited
- Walking distance from Quepos hotels is ~7 km or take local bus (₡300/~$0.50)

### Marina Pez Vela
**GPS:** 9.4178, -84.1639

Modern marina facility in Quepos offering sportfishing, dining, and shopping. Good option for a sunset dinner with harbor views.

### Damas Island Estuary
**GPS:** 9.4583, -84.2000

Just 15-20 minutes from Quepos, this mangrove estuary ecosystem offers kayaking and boat tours to see crocodiles, monkeys, sloths, and diverse birdlife. A more relaxed alternative to the busy national park.

## Stage 122: Quepos to Palmar Norte
**Sunday, December 14, 2025**
**Distance:** 106 km | **Elevation:** +1,007 m / -987 m

This stage follows the spectacular Costanera Sur (Route 34) along Costa Rica's southern Pacific coast, passing through the Costa Ballena region before turning inland. The route offers dramatic ocean views, surfing towns, and access to unique coastal ecosystems.

### Route Notes
The Costanera Sur is a well-maintained highway with rolling terrain. Expect warm, humid conditions at sea level. The route passes through several small towns with services. After Dominical, the highway continues south through Uvita before reaching the Palmar Norte turnoff. Watch for afternoon rain showers typical in December.

### Key Attractions Along Route

#### Dominical
**GPS:** 9.2517, -83.8592
**Distance from start:** ~44 km

A laid-back surf town known for consistent waves and bohemian atmosphere. Good stop for lunch and refreshments. The town has become a hub for adventure activities including waterfall tours, surfing lessons, and jungle excursions.

**Nearby:** Nauyaca Waterfall - one of Costa Rica's most impressive waterfalls. Two cascading falls with a swimming pool below. Located in the jungle north of Dominical (requires hiking or horseback tour).

#### Marino Ballena National Park (Whale Tail)
**GPS:** 9.1561, -83.7514
**Distance from start:** ~55 km

Costa Rica's first marine national park, famous for its unique whale-tail-shaped sandbar (Punta Uvita) visible at low tide. Created in 1992 to protect humpback whale breeding grounds.

**Highlights:**
- Walk on the "Whale's Tail" tombolo at low tide
- December falls within whale migration season (humpbacks from Northern Hemisphere)
- 9 miles of protected coastline
- Excellent snorkeling around the tombolo and offshore islands
- Multiple beach access points: Uvita, Colonia, Ballena, Piñuelas

**Practical information:**
- Entry: $6 USD
- Hours: 7 AM - 6 PM
- Walking distance to whale tail from Uvita entrance: ~2 km (30-40 min)

**Note for cyclists:** This is a worthwhile detour if your rest day timing allows, but adds distance to the stage. Check tide charts for best viewing times.

#### Ballena Islands
**GPS:** 9.1442, -83.7311

Three small rock formations offshore, visible from the coast. Home to nesting seabirds and surrounded by coral reefs. Accessible via boat tours from Uvita.

#### Ojochal
**GPS:** 9.0750, -83.6667
**Distance from start:** ~70 km

A small expat community known for having some of the best international restaurants in Costa Rica. Good lunch stop with French, fusion, and local cuisine options.

#### Térraba-Sierpe National Wetlands
**GPS:** 8.9500, -83.5500
**Distance from start:** ~85 km

Costa Rica's largest wetland system covering approximately 24,730 hectares. The mangrove-lined channels are rich in wildlife including crocodiles, caimans, and diverse birdlife. The Sierpe River is the gateway to Drake Bay and the Osa Peninsula.

### Palmar Norte Area Attractions

#### Finca 6 (Stone Spheres UNESCO Site)
**GPS:** 8.9110, -83.4773
**Near Palmar Norte/Sierpe junction**

One of Costa Rica's most mysterious archaeological sites and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2014. Part of the "Precolumbian Chiefdom Settlements with Stone Spheres of the Diquís."

**What you'll see:**
- Pre-Columbian stone spheres dating from 500-1500 CE
- Some spheres reach 2.57 m in diameter and weigh up to 16 tons
- Museum with exhibits on the Diquís culture
- Three walking trails (175m to 1.2 km)
- Remnants of United Fruit Company banana plantation infrastructure

**Historical context:**
The spheres were created by the now-extinct Diquís culture. Their purpose remains unknown - theories include astronomical markers, status symbols, or territorial markers. They were discovered in the 1930s when United Fruit Company cleared land for banana plantations.

**Practical information:**
- Hours: Tuesday-Sunday, 8 AM - 4 PM
- Entry: $6 USD adults, $4 students
- Location: 7 km from Palmar Norte toward Sierpe on Route 223
- Allow 1-2 hours for visit
- Contact: Museo Nacional de Costa Rica

#### Palmar Sur Stone Spheres Park
**GPS:** 8.9500, -83.4800

Additional stone spheres displayed in a park across from the Palmar Sur airport. Includes two large spheres (2.10 m diameter) that were famously saved from removal by local high school students who blocked the highway in protest.

#### Sierpe River Gateway
**GPS:** 8.8658, -83.4839

Small town of Sierpe, departure point for boats to Drake Bay and Corcovado National Park. The mangrove-lined river channels are excellent for wildlife watching.

## Stage 123: Palmar Norte to San Vito
**Monday, December 15, 2025**
**Distance:** 82 km | **Elevation:** +1,964 m / -995 m

The most challenging climbing day of this block, ascending from the Pacific lowlands into the Coto Brus highlands. This stage crosses from tropical heat into pleasant mountain climate as you climb to ~1,100 m elevation.

### Route Notes
This is a demanding stage with nearly 2,000 m of climbing. The route follows the Inter-American Highway (Route 2) to Paso Real, then climbs into the Coto Brus Valley on Route 237. Start early to avoid afternoon heat and rain. Temperatures cool significantly as you gain elevation. The final approach to San Vito passes through coffee plantations and offers beautiful valley views.

### Key Attractions Along Route

#### Paso Real Junction
**GPS:** 9.0000, -83.3500
**Distance from start:** ~30 km

Important junction where the route turns off the Inter-American Highway toward San Vito. The bridge over the Río Grande de Térraba marks the transition from coastal lowlands to mountain terrain.

#### Térraba River Valley
The Térraba River is Costa Rica's longest and largest river by volume. The valley formed by tectonic activity extends from San Isidro to Panama. Watch for the dramatic transition in landscape as you climb.

#### La Amistad Biosphere Reserve Buffer Zone
**GPS:** 8.9500, -83.1000

As you climb toward San Vito, you enter the buffer zone of the UNESCO-designated La Amistad Biosphere Reserve. This massive protected area spans both Costa Rica and Panama, harboring extraordinary biodiversity including jaguars, tapirs, and the resplendent quetzal.

### San Vito - Italian Heritage Town

#### Centro Cultural Dante Alighieri
**GPS:** 8.8215, -82.9708
**Central San Vito**

Cultural center documenting the fascinating history of Italian immigration to San Vito. Located across from the central park.

**Historical context:**
San Vito was founded in 1952 by Italian immigrants fleeing post-WWII economic devastation. The Italian Agricultural Colonization Society (SICA) purchased 10,000 hectares from the Costa Rican government. Around 500 Italian colonists from 40 different regions - from Trieste to Taranto - arrived to carve farms from virgin forest. They built roads, bridges, schools, an electric plant, and an airstrip. The coffee industry they developed transformed the region.

Today, San Vito remains Costa Rica's most Italian town. About 3,000 of the 5,000 inhabitants are of Italian descent. Italian is taught as a compulsory subject in local schools.

**Cultural highlights:**
- Italian restaurants with authentic cuisine (try Liliana, La Casa Italiana, Le Crepes)
- Italian-style bakeries and pastry shops (Panaderia Flor)
- Catholic church with Italian community center
- Notably, bread is more common than tortillas here due to Italian influence

#### Wilson Botanical Garden (Las Cruces)
**GPS:** 8.7867, -82.9617
**6 km south of San Vito center**

One of Central America's most important botanical gardens and a world-class destination for plant enthusiasts and birdwatchers. Part of the Organization for Tropical Studies (OTS) Las Cruces Biological Station.

**What you'll see:**
- Over 1,000 genera in 200+ plant families
- Second largest palm collection in the world (~500 species)
- Exceptional collections of heliconias, bromeliads, orchids, ferns
- The only remaining primary forest fragment in the region (365 hectares)
- 425+ bird species recorded including resplendent quetzal
- 800+ butterfly species

**History:**
Founded in 1963 by Robert and Catherine Wilson, who moved from Miami, Florida. Inspired by legendary Brazilian landscape architect Roberto Burle Marx. Transferred to OTS in 1973.

**Trails:**
- Multiple garden paths through themed collections
- Seven forest trails covering 12 km
- Rio Java Trail to canopy tower for birdwatching

**Practical information:**
- Guided walks: 7:30 AM and 1:30 PM, Monday-Saturday
- Entry: ~$10 USD for garden; ~$95/person for lodging with meals and tour
- Hours: 8 AM - 4 PM
- Contact: +506 2773-4004, lascruces@tropicalstudies.org
- Accommodation available in 12 lodge-style cabins

#### San Vito viewpoints
The roads around San Vito offer spectacular views of the Coto Brus Valley and surrounding mountains. On clear days you can see into Panama.

## Stage 124: San Vito to Volcan (Costa Rica-Panama Border Crossing)
**Tuesday, December 16, 2025**
**Distance:** 53 km | **Elevation:** +1,550 m / -1,187 m

The shortest stage of this block takes you across the international border from Costa Rica into Panama's Chiriquí Highlands. Despite the short distance, significant climbing remains as you ascend toward Volcan at ~1,300-1,500 m elevation.

### Route Notes
This stage crosses from Costa Rica to Panama - ensure passports and any required documentation are readily accessible. The border crossing is straightforward but allow time for formalities. Note that Panama is ONE HOUR AHEAD of Costa Rica.

The route continues through mountain terrain with coffee plantations and cloud forest. Temperatures remain pleasant due to elevation. The final destination, Volcan, is a charming highland town on the slopes of Panama's highest peak.

### Border Crossing Information

#### Rio Sereno-San Vito Border
**GPS:** 8.7833, -82.8833

This is a quieter, less chaotic border crossing than the busy Paso Canoas crossing to the south. Located in mountainous terrain between San Vito, Costa Rica and Volcan, Panama.

**Requirements (verify current requirements before travel):**
- Valid passport (not expiring within 6 months)
- Proof of onward travel (bus ticket or flight confirmation)
- Proof of funds ($500 or bank statement)
- May require hotel reservation in Panama
- Costa Rica exit tax: ~$8 USD

**Tips:**
- Tour operators typically handle border crossing logistics
- Have documents easily accessible
- Change time on watches/devices (Panama +1 hour)
- Cell phone coverage may change - consider local SIM

### Panama's Chiriquí Highlands

#### Volcán Barú National Park
**GPS:** 8.8078, -82.5417

Panama's highest point (3,474 m / 11,398 ft) and only volcano dominates the landscape. The park protects cloud forests, páramo highlands, and the volcanic crater.

**Key facts:**
- 12th highest peak in Central America
- Only place on Earth where you can see both Atlantic and Pacific oceans on a clear day
- Five different ecosystems due to altitudinal variation
- Last eruption estimated 400-500 years ago (considered dormant but active)
- Home to resplendent quetzals and hundreds of other bird species
- Seven crater formations at summit

**Access from Volcan:**
- Multiple hiking trails from Volcan, Cerro Punta, and Boquete
- Paso Ancho route: 7-12 hours hiking
- 4x4 vehicle tours available to summit
- Best conditions: December-April (dry season)

**Practical information:**
- Park entrance fee required
- Temperatures at summit can drop below 0°C (bring layers)
- Start pre-dawn for sunrise summit views
- Guide recommended due to unmarked trails

#### Lagunas de Volcán
**GPS:** 8.8300, -82.6500

Beautiful natural lagoons on the outskirts of Volcan town, maintained by the Janson family on private property. Popular for hiking, fishing, horseback riding, and camping with stunning mountain views.

#### La Amistad International Park (Panama sector)
**GPS:** 8.8500, -82.7500

The Panama portion of this UNESCO World Heritage Site is accessible from the Volcan/Cerro Punta area. Covering nearly 1 million acres across both countries, La Amistad ("Friendship") represents one of the first transboundary parks in the Americas.

**Biodiversity:**
- 600+ bird species
- 215 mammal species including jaguars, tapirs, and pumas
- Indigenous communities (Naso, Bribri, Ngöbe-Buglé) within and near the park
- Cloud forests, alpine páramo, glacial lakes

**Access:**
- Las Nubes entrance near Cerro Punta
- Trails ranging from easy walks to multi-day expeditions
- Guide recommended - trails not well documented

#### Sendero Los Quetzales
One of Panama's most famous hiking trails, connecting Boquete with Cerro Punta around the flanks of Volcán Barú. Named for the resplendent quetzal commonly seen along the route. Allow a full day for the ~8 km trail.

## Rest Day: Volcan (Wednesday, December 17, 2025)

Volcan is a charming highland town sometimes called "Little Switzerland" for its European-style architecture and cool mountain climate. Settled by Swiss, German, and Yugoslav immigrants before road access existed, the town retains its distinctive character.

### Recommended Activities

#### Coffee Farm Tours
The Chiriquí Highlands produce world-renowned Geisha coffee - one of the world's most expensive varietals. The volcanic soil and high altitude (up to 1,900 m) create ideal growing conditions.

Several fincas offer tours showcasing the cultivation, harvesting, and processing of this premium coffee. A unique opportunity to taste some of the world's finest coffee at its source.

#### Finca Ceriana
**GPS:** 8.8000, -82.6800

Private nature reserve in the highlands/lowlands transition zone. Conservation-focused eco-tourism with excellent wildlife viewing opportunities.

#### Hot Springs
Natural thermal springs in the area offer relaxation after days of climbing. Caldera Hot Springs near Boquete is a popular option.

#### Birdwatching
The Volcan area is exceptional for birdwatching, particularly for the resplendent quetzal. December falls within prime quetzal season. Local guides can arrange early morning expeditions to reliable viewing spots.

#### Mountain Biking and Hiking
Well-suited terrain for off-bike exploration. The cool climate makes hiking pleasant. Multiple trails of varying difficulty access cloud forests and volcanic landscapes.

### Practical Information for Volcan

**Climate:** Average temperatures 14-27°C (57-80°F). Bring warm layers for evenings and early mornings. Can drop below 10°C at night in December.

**Services:** Banks, ATMs, supermarkets, restaurants, and hotels available. Good infrastructure for a small town.

**Currency:** US Dollar (Panama uses USD)

**Time Zone:** UTC-5 (1 hour ahead of Costa Rica)

## Regional Context

### Geographic Setting
This route traverses one of Central America's most biodiverse regions, from Pacific lowlands through the Talamanca-Chiriquí mountain range. The dramatic elevation changes create distinct life zones, from tropical wet forest at sea level to cloud forest and páramo at high elevations.

### Conservation Significance
The La Amistad Biosphere Reserve connecting Costa Rica and Panama represents the largest protected area in Central America. Combined with adjacent parks (Volcán Barú, Chirripó), it forms a crucial biological corridor allowing species movement between North and South America.

### Cultural Heritage
The route passes through areas of rich indigenous history (Diquís stone spheres, Brunca peoples) and more recent immigrant heritage (Italian colonization of San Vito, European settlement of Chiriquí Highlands). This blend creates unique cultural experiences from food to architecture to language.

### December Weather Considerations
- Pacific lowlands: Hot and humid, possible afternoon showers
- Highlands above 1,000 m: Pleasant temperatures, occasional rain
- Morning starts recommended for heat management
- Humpback whale migration active (December is within Northern Hemisphere migration window)

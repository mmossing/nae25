## Stage 96: Mexico City to Cholula (66 km)
**Wednesday, November 12, 2025**

### Major Highlights

#### **Great Pyramid of Cholula (Tlachihualtépetl)**
- **Significance**: The largest pyramid by volume in the world (4.45 million cubic meters)
- **Size**: 300 x 315 meters base, 25 meters high above plain
- **History**: Built in four stages from 3rd century BC through 9th century AD
- **Access**: Over 5 miles of tunnels open to visitors
- **Current Use**: Crowned with the baroque Iglesia de Nuestra Señora de los Remedios (Church of Our Lady of Remedies)
- **Cultural Context**: Known in Nahuatl as "hand-made mountain" - locals believed it was built by humans, not gods

#### **Puebla City (Adjacent to Cholula)**
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site**: Historic center recognized for colonial architecture
- **Volcanic Views**: Framed by Popocatépetl, Iztaccíhuatl, and La Malinche volcanoes
- **Architecture**: Spectacular neoclassical and baroque buildings
- **Cuisine**: Famous for mole poblano and traditional Pueblan dishes
- **Cycling Connection**: Ciclopista Angelopolitana - a dedicated bike path connecting Cholula to Puebla's Paseo del Rio Atoyac

### Route Notes
- **Elevation Profile**: Starting elevation ~2,240m (Mexico City) to ~2,160m (Cholula)
- **Terrain**: Mix of urban and rural, passing through Valley of Mexico
- **Note**: KML file indicates "by bus" - this may be a transfer or optional cycling route
- **Unpaved Sections**: 20-40% unpaved terrain

### Points of Interest En Route
1. **Volcanic Corridor**: Route passes through the shadow of major volcanoes
2. **Traditional Villages**: Several small pueblos between CDMX and Puebla
3. **Cerro Zapotecas**: Dormant volcano visible from Cholula with hiking trails

## Stage 98: Ixcaquixtla to Huajuapan de León (100 km)
**Friday, November 14, 2025**

### Destination: Huajuapan de León

#### **Historical Significance**
- **Full Name**: Heroica Ciudad de Huajuapan de León
- **Mixtec Name**: Ñuu dee ("Place of Brave People")
- **Elevation**: ~1,600m
- **Population**: ~78,000 (6th largest in Oaxaca state)

#### **Historical Events**
- **The Siege of Huajuapan (1812)**: 111-day siege during Mexican War of Independence
  - Resistance led by Coronel Valerio Trujano with support from General José María Morelos
  - Town elevated to honorary status in June 1843 to commemorate the battle
- **Named After**: Antonio de León, hero of the Mexican War of Independence

#### **Major Attractions**

**1. Cerro de las Minas Archaeological Site**
- **Culture**: Ñuiñe (Mixtec lowland culture)
- **Period**: 400-800 CE (contemporary with Monte Albán and Teotihuacan)
- **Features**: Only extensively excavated and public archaeological site in the Mixteca Baja
- **Includes**: Ball courts, palaces, temples, public spaces, residential areas

**2. Cathedral of Nuestra Señora de Guadalupe**
- **Established**: Diocese seat since 1903
- **Architecture**: Neoclassical style with 17th century origins
- **Multiple expansions**: Throughout 19th century

**3. Capilla del Señor de los Corazones (Chapel of Our Lord of the Hearts)**
- **Legend**: Image from 17th century, destined for Veracruz coast
- **Miracle**: Became too heavy to move when passing through Huajuapan
- **Attribution**: Credited with "breaking" the 1812 siege

**4. Regional Museum (MUREH)**
- **Founded**: January 19, 1999
- **Focus**: Mixtec archaeology, ethnography, and history
- **Collections**: Permanent and temporary exhibitions
- **Additional**: Library, bookstore, artisan crafts

**5. Cerro del Yukunitza (Glass Lookout)**
- **Feature**: Panoramic viewpoint overlooking the city
- **Access**: Via beautiful hiking trails
- **Climate Note**: Arid, desert-like climate - limited shade

#### **Cultural Notes**
- **Birthplace**: José López Alavés, composer of the world-famous "Mixteca" song (born July 14, 1889)
- **Ethnic Heritage**: Predominantly Mixtec descent, though language not commonly spoken in city
- **Economic**: High migration to United States; remittances important to local economy

#### **Geography & Climate**
- **Region**: Mixteca Baja (part of larger Mixteca region)
- **Terrain**: Rugged, mountainous area
- **Climate**: Warm humid subtropical (Köppen Cwa); dry most of year
- **Major Highway Junction**: Federal Highways 125 and 190

## Stage 100: Asunción Nochixtlán to Oaxaca (106 km)
**Sunday, November 16, 2025**

### Final Approach to Oaxaca
- **Terrain**: Descending from Mixteca Alta into Oaxaca Valley
- **Dramatic Descent**: 2,015m of elevation loss
- **Landscape Transition**: From high mountains to valley floor

### Destination: Oaxaca de Juárez

#### **Overview**
- **Full Name**: Oaxaca de Juárez
- **Elevation**: ~1,550m
- **Population**: ~265,000 (city); ~4 million (state)
- **UNESCO Status**: Historic Center designated World Heritage Site (1987) along with Monte Albán

#### **Cultural Significance**
- **Indigenous Heritage**: Home to 16 distinct ethnic groups
- **Languages**: Over 16 indigenous languages spoken
- **Two Largest Groups**: Zapotec and Mixtec peoples
- **Cultural Capital**: One of Mexico's most culturally rich cities

#### **Major Attractions**

**1. Historic Center (Centro Histórico)**
- **Zócalo**: Main plaza, heart of city life
- **Architecture**: Stunning colonial buildings in green cantera stone
- **UNESCO Recognition**: One of best-preserved colonial centers in Americas

**2. Santo Domingo de Guzmán Church and Cultural Center**
- **Construction**: 16th-17th century baroque masterpiece
- **Interior**: Elaborately decorated with gold leaf
- **Adjacent**: Ethnobotanical Garden showcasing native Oaxacan plants

**3. Monte Albán Archaeological Site**
- **Distance**: ~9 km from city center
- **Culture**: Zapotec capital
- **Period**: Founded ~500 BCE, peak 200-850 CE
- **UNESCO Status**: World Heritage Site
- **Features**: Great Plaza, ball court, astronomical observatory, tombs with frescoes
- **Significance**: One of Mesoamerica's first major urban centers

**4. Markets**
- **Mercado 20 de Noviembre**: Famous for food, especially tlayudas and tasajo
- **Mercado Benito Juárez**: Traditional crafts, textiles, local products
- **Abastos Market**: Largest market, authentic local experience

**5. Museums**
- **Museo de las Culturas de Oaxaca**: In former Santo Domingo monastery
- **Museo Rufino Tamayo**: Pre-Hispanic art collection
- **Textile Museum of Oaxaca**: Indigenous weaving traditions

#### **Cuisine**
- **Seven Moles**: Oaxaca famous for seven distinct mole varieties
- **Tlayudas**: Large crispy tortillas with toppings
- **Chapulines**: Toasted grasshoppers (seasonal delicacy)
- **Mezcal**: Traditional distilled agave spirit (Santiago Matatlán, the "World Capital of Mezcal," is nearby)
- **Chocolate**: Traditional Oaxacan chocolate and hot chocolate
- **Quesillo**: Oaxacan string cheese

#### **Artisan Traditions**
- **Alebrijes**: Colorful carved wooden creatures
- **Black Pottery**: Traditional barro negro from San Bartolo Coyotepec
- **Textiles**: Hand-woven rugs, garments with natural dyes
- **Mezcal Production**: Traditional palenques (distilleries) in surrounding villages

#### **Nearby Villages (Craft Villages)**
- **Teotitlán del Valle**: Hand-woven wool rugs using natural dyes
- **San Bartolo Coyotepec**: Black pottery (barro negro)
- **San Martín Tilcajete**: Alebrijes (carved wooden creatures)
- **Santa María Atzompa**: Green-glazed ceramics
- **Arrazola**: Another alebrije center
- **Ocotlán de Morelos**: Friday market, known for pottery and textiles

#### **Archaeological Sites (Beyond Monte Albán)**
- **Mitla**: Zapotec site with geometric stone mosaics (~45 km east)
- **Yagul**: Hilltop fortress and ball court
- **Dainzú**: Ancient ball court reliefs
- **Atzompa**: Recently opened site with views over Oaxaca valley
- **Zaachila**: Former Zapotec capital with intact tombs

4. **Frida Kahlo Museum (Casa Azul)**: In Coyoacán neighborhood

### **Oaxaca - November 17, 2025 (Monday)**

#### **Revolution Day**
- **Official Holiday**: Third Monday of November (November 17, 2025)
- **Celebrations**: May include parades, civic ceremonies, cultural events
- **Historical Significance**: Commemorates the 1910 Mexican Revolution

#### **Post-Día de Muertos Period**
- **Timing**: You'll arrive November 16, two weeks after Day of the Dead
- **Note**: Major Muertos celebrations (Oct 31-Nov 2) will be over
- **Lingering**: Some decorations may still be visible, altars in museums
- **Markets**: Back to normal operations

#### **What to Expect in Mid-November**
1. **Weather**: Pleasant temperatures, dry season beginning
2. **Tourism Level**: Moderate (post-Muertos rush, pre-December holidays)
3. **Markets**: Excellent time to visit without peak crowds
4. **Cultural Events**: Regular programming of concerts, exhibitions

#### **Recommended Rest Day Activities**

**Historical/Cultural:**
1. Visit Monte Albán archaeological site early morning (best light, fewer crowds)
2. Explore Santo Domingo Church and Ethnobotanical Garden
3. Cultural museums (Cultures of Oaxaca, Textile Museum, Rufino Tamayo)
4. Walking tour of historic center and zócalo

**Markets & Food:**
1. Morning visit to Mercado 20 de Noviembre for breakfast
2. Mercado Benito Juárez for crafts and local products
3. Chocolate tasting and traditional hot chocolate
4. Mole tasting experience (seven varieties)
5. Mezcal tasting at local mezcalería

**Craft Villages (Day Trips):**
1. Teotitlán del Valle: Weaving demonstrations, natural dyes
2. San Bartolo Coyotepec: Black pottery workshops
3. Hierve el Agua: Petrified waterfalls (~70 km, full day trip)

**Relaxation:**
1. Café hopping in historic center
2. Sunset from rooftop bars overlooking Santo Domingo
3. Traditional temazcal (steam bath) experience

## Regional Context: The Mixteca

### **Three Sub-Regions Along Your Route**

#### **Mixteca Baja (Low Mixteca)**
- **Elevation**: ~1,000-1,800m
- **Climate**: Warm, dry, semi-arid
- **Your Route**: Ixcaquixtla, Huajuapan de León
- **Characteristics**: Desert-like landscape, rugged hills, limited water

#### **Mixteca Alta (High Mixteca)**
- **Elevation**: ~2,000-3,000m
- **Climate**: Cooler, some rainfall
- **Your Route**: Asunción Nochixtlán
- **Characteristics**: Pine forests, cooler temperatures, agricultural terraces

#### **Valles Centrales (Central Valleys)**
- **Elevation**: ~1,500-1,800m
- **Climate**: Temperate, dry season Nov-May
- **Your Route**: Oaxaca de Juárez
- **Characteristics**: Broad valley floor, agricultural, population center

### **Mixtec Cultural Heritage**
- **Name Origin**: Mixtec = "People of the Rain" or "Cloud People"
- **Language Family**: Mixtecan languages (part of Oto-Manguean family)
- **Archaeological Legacy**: Known for elaborate tombs, codices, goldwork
- **Historical Period**: Flourished 1000-1521 CE (Classic and Postclassic periods)
- **Contemporary**: Communities maintain language, traditions, crafts
- **Diaspora**: Significant migration to United States, especially California

## Practical Cycling Considerations

### **Elevation Profile Summary**
- **Stage 96**: Relatively flat, high elevation (~2,200m)
- **Stage 97**: Longest stage, gradual descent
- **Stage 98**: Moderate, continuing descent to ~1,600m
- **Stage 99**: Highest climbing day (1,715m gain), ascending to ~2,100m
- **Stage 100**: Epic descent (2,015m loss) into Oaxaca valley

### **Road Conditions**
- **Stage 96**: 20-40% unpaved (highest unpaved percentage in this section)
- **Stages 97-100**: Mostly paved (<1% unpaved)
- **Primary Route**: Federal Highway 190 for much of stages 98-100
- **Traffic**: Moderate on Federal 190; expect trucks and buses

### **Weather Expectations (Mid-November)**
- **Season**: Beginning of dry season
- **Temperatures**: 
  - Highs: 20-25°C (68-77°F) in valleys
  - Lows: 5-10°C (41-50°F) at higher elevations
- **Precipitation**: Minimal, dry season established
- **Sun**: Strong UV exposure at altitude
- **Wind**: Possible afternoon winds in valley areas

### **Altitude Considerations**
- **Starting Elevation**: 2,200-2,400m (Mexico City/Cholula)
- **Highest Point**: ~2,100m (Asunción Nochixtlán area)
- **Finishing Elevation**: ~1,550m (Oaxaca)
- **Acclimatization**: Should be well-acclimatized by this point in tour
- **Hydration**: Critical due to altitude and dry climate

### **Cultural & Logistical Notes**
- **Language**: Spanish primary; indigenous languages common in rural areas
- **ATMs**: Available in major towns (Cholula, Huajuapan, Oaxaca)
- **Food**: Increasing availability of regional Oaxacan cuisine
- **Accommodations**: Range from basic to mid-range in intermediate towns
- **Cell Service**: Generally good on Federal Highway 190

## Historical Timeline Context

### **Your Arrival Dates in Historical Context**

#### **Mexico City (November 9-11)**
- **November 8, 1519**: Hernán Cortés and Moctezuma II meet in Tenochtitlan
  - Your arrival is 506 years and ~3 days after this historic encounter
  - This meeting began the series of events leading to the fall of the Aztec Empire

#### **Cholula (November 12)**
- **Site of 1519 Cholula Massacre**: Shortly before Cortés reached Tenochtitlan
- **Spanish Colonial Period**: Church built atop pyramid symbolizes religious conquest

#### **Route Through Mixteca (November 13-16)**
- **Pre-Hispanic Period**: Peak of Mixtec civilization (1000-1521 CE)
- **War of Independence**: Huajuapan's famous 1812 siege
- **Modern Era**: Cultural continuity despite colonial and modern pressures

#### **Oaxaca (November 16-17)**
- **Zapotec Origins**: Monte Albán founded ~500 BCE
- **Mixtec Period**: Occupied Oaxaca Valley after Zapotec decline
- **Spanish Conquest**: Oaxaca founded 1529
- **Famous Residents**: Benito Juárez (Mexico's first indigenous president) from nearby Guelatao

## References & Resources

### **Archaeological Sites**
- INAH (Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia) manages sites
- Many sites closed Mondays
- Arrive early for best light and fewer crowds
- Bring sun protection and water

### **Cultural Sensitivity**
- Ask permission before photographing people
- Respect indigenous communities and traditions
- Markets: vendors appreciate purchases before extensive photography
- Sacred sites: Follow posted guidelines
- Indigenous languages: Respect linguistic diversity

### **Recommended Reading**
- "The Cloud People" - Mixtec history and archaeology
- "Oaxaca Journal" by Oliver Sacks - naturalist's perspective
- "Oaxaca: The Archaeological Record" - comprehensive site guide
- Mixtec codices - pre-Hispanic painted manuscripts

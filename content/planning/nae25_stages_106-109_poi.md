## Stage 106: Tapachula to Coatepeque (82 km)
**Monday, November 24, 2025**

### Border Crossing Day

This stage takes you from Mexico's southernmost major city across the international border into Guatemala. The route follows the Pacific coastal region before turning inland through rich agricultural lands. You'll transition from the Mexican state of Chiapas into Guatemala's coffee-growing highlands.

### Pre-Departure: Tapachula Highlights

**Izapa Archaeological Zone**
- Location: 14°55'23"N, 92°10'47"W (13 km from Tapachula)
- One of the oldest Mesoamerican ceremonial centers (1500 BCE - 1200 CE)
- Bridge between Olmec and Maya civilizations
- Famous Stela 5 "Tree of Life" depicts Maya creation mythology from Popol Vuh
- 89 carved stelae, 61 altars, aligned with Tacana Volcano
- Open Wed-Sun 8 AM-5 PM, $4.20/70 pesos entrance
- Cultural significance: Possible origin of the 260-day Maya calendar

**Coffee Plantation Tours**
- *Finca Argovia*: German-founded 1880 plantation with boutique hotel, organic coffee, 187 hectares of rainforest
- *Finca Hamburgo*: Founded 1888, elevation 1,249m with views of Volcán Tacaná, coffee museum
- Both fincas offer tours, tastings, and insight into the "Ruta del Café" coffee route heritage
- German immigrant history dates to the late 1800s coffee boom

**Tapachula City Center**
- Casa de Cultura: 1929 Art Deco municipal palace with Oaxacan fretwork
- Parque Central/Parque Hidalgo: Heart of the city
- Church of San Agustín: Prominent colonial landmark
- Markets: Mercado Sebastián Escobar (with Chinese culinary influence), Mercado Soconusco, Mercado San Juan
- Notable mix of cultures: German, Chinese, Lebanese, Maya, and Nahua communities

### Along the Route

**Mexico-Guatemala Border**
- Main crossing at Ciudad Hidalgo (40 km from Tapachula)
- Suchiate River marks international boundary
- Have passport, visa ready; expect standard border formalities
- Note: Guatemala is in Central Standard Time (CST, UTC-6), same as Mexico during non-daylight saving periods

### Arrival: Coatepeque, Guatemala

**Lake Coatepeque** (Nearby)
- Stunning volcanic crater lake, one of Central America's largest
- Elevation: Approximately 700m
- Activities: kayaking, swimming, fishing, boating
- Panoramic viewpoint: Mirador del Cerro El Baúl

**Coffee Region Context**
- Coatepeque is a major coffee-growing center, one of Guatemala's richest agricultural areas
- Historic role as Mayan trading center pre-conquest
- Founded by Spanish conquistadors under Pedro de Alvarado in 1525

**Regional History**
- Area inhabited by ancient Maya civilization
- Violent 1902 eruption of Santa María volcano caused widespread destruction in western Guatemala
- Modern Coatepeque is an important commercial hub on the Pacific Coast Highway

### Riding Notes

- Mostly paved roads with < 1% unpaved sections
- Moderate 971m of elevation gain
- Hot, tropical conditions in the morning (Tapachula elevation: 116m)
- Afternoon arrival at higher, cooler Coatepeque (465m elevation)
- Border crossing may add time - start early
- Carry extra water due to heat and humidity

## Stage 107: Coatepeque to Quetzaltenango (63 km)
**Tuesday, November 25, 2025**

### The Big Climb

This is one of the most challenging stages of your entire tour: 63 km with a massive 2,493 meters of elevation gain. You'll climb from 465m in Coatepeque to 2,340m in Quetzaltenango, ascending into Guatemala's Western Highlands through coffee plantations and cloud forests.

### What Makes This Climb Special

Despite the difficulty, you're rewarded with:
- Spectacular views of volcanic landscapes
- Transition through multiple ecological zones
- Arrival in Guatemala's authentic highland Maya heartland
- Cool mountain climate (a relief after the coastal heat)

### Arrival: Quetzaltenango (Xela)

**Understanding the Name**
- Official name: Quetzaltenango ("Place of the Quetzal Bird")
- Commonly called "Xela" (pronounced "Shay-la" or "Shel-la")
- Indigenous name: Xelajú Noj ("Under Ten Mountains/Gods")
- Guatemala's second-largest city, elevation 2,340m

**Cultural Significance**
- 95% indigenous Maya (primarily K'iche' Maya)
- Considered the "unofficial capital of the Maya"
- Colonial-style historic center with traditional markets
- Less touristy than Antigua - authentic Guatemalan experience
- Colonial name commemorates the K'iche' ruler Tecún Umán, defeated by Spanish conquistador Pedro de Alvarado in 1524

**Attractions in Xela**

*Central Historic District*
- Parque Central/Central Park: Main plaza with colonial architecture
- Teatro Municipal: Beautiful neoclassical theatre built 1895, known for jazz performances
- Cathedral: Historic colonial church on main square
- Pasaje Enríquez: Historic arcade with Salón Tecún restaurant/bar (established 1934)
- Casa Noj No'j: Cultural center promoting art and culture (Mayan nawal of intelligence)

*Museums & Cultural Sites*
- Museo Regional de Occidente: Western Guatemala history and culture
- Art galleries throughout city center
- Traditional markets with colorful Maya textiles and produce

*Nearby Natural Attractions*
- **Volcán Santa María** (3,770m): Perfect cone volcano, towering over the city
- **Volcán Santiaguito** (active): Visible eruptions from viewpoint, sunrise hikes available
- **Laguna Chicabal**: Sacred crater lake atop volcano, Mayan ceremonial site
- **Fuentes Georginas**: Natural thermal hot springs from Zunil Volcano

*Day Trip to Salcaja* (9 km away)
- La Conquistadora: First cathedral in Central America (built 1524)
- Famous for "Rompope" - fermented fruit broth with local rum

**Food & Culture**
- Xelapan: Most popular bakery and café, located in Parque Central
- Traditional Guatemalan restaurants: Tertulianos, Restaurante Panorama
- Highland cuisine features pepián (meat stew), tamales, local corn dishes
- Strong coffee culture - this is premium coffee-growing country

**Climate Advisory**
- Cold at night in November, temperatures 5-14°C (40-57°F)
- Bring warm layers/jacket for evening
- Days are pleasant and mild
- Much cooler than the coastal regions you've left behind

### Riding Notes

- This is a SERIOUS climb: 2,493m gain over just 63 km
- Pace yourself - this may take 5-7 hours
- Stock up on food and water
- Route passes through coffee plantations
- Spectacular scenery as reward for the effort
- Cool temperatures at altitude - layer clothing
- Minimal unpaved sections (< 1%)

## Stage 108: Quetzaltenango to Santiago Atitlán (103 km)
**Wednesday, November 26, 2025**

### To the Sacred Lake

After yesterday's monster climb, today brings another substantial ascent (2,250m) but also one of the most spectacular destinations in all of Central America: Lake Atitlán, often called the most beautiful lake in the world. This stage takes you through Maya villages and highland terrain before descending to the stunning lake surrounded by three towering volcanoes.

### Lake Atitlán: The Jewel of Guatemala

**Geographic Wonders**
- Elevation: 1,562m above sea level
- Maximum depth: 340m - deepest lake in Central America
- Formed in a massive volcanic caldera
- Surrounded by three guardian volcanoes:
  - **Volcán San Pedro** (3,020m)
  - **Volcán Tolimán** (3,158m) 
  - **Volcán Atitlán** (3,535m - active but last erupted 1856)
- 11 lakeside villages, each with unique character
- Sacred to the Maya for thousands of years

**Climate & Conditions**
- Perfect temperatures: 21-27°C (70s-80s F) days, 10-20°C (50s-60s F) nights
- Dry season November-April (you'll arrive during ideal conditions)
- Spectacular mountain and volcano views
- November is one of the best months to visit

### Arrival: Santiago Atitlán

**About Santiago Atitlán**
- Largest traditional town on Lake Atitlán
- Bastion of Tz'utujil Maya culture
- Location: 14.637°N, 91.227°W, elevation 1,568m
- Population maintains strong indigenous traditions
- Access to town is primarily by boat (lanchas) from other lake villages

**Cultural & Historical Significance**

*Colonial Heritage*
- Parroquia Santiago Apóstol: Beautiful colonial church, focal point of community
- 16th-century church with colonial architecture and religious artifacts
- Church dates to Spanish colonial period, rebuilt/modified over centuries

*Maya Traditions*
- Traditional Tz'utujil Maya textiles and weaving
- Women's cooperatives offer weaving demonstrations and workshops
- Colorful handwoven huipiles (traditional blouses) and textiles in markets
- Traditional fishing techniques passed down through generations

*Maximón (San Simón)*
- Unique folk saint blending Maya and Catholic traditions
- Shrine moves to different house each year
- Offerings include tobacco, alcohol, candles
- Guided tours explain spiritual significance
- Important in local religious syncretism

*Civil War Memorial*
- Parque de Paz: Memorial to 1990 massacre where 14 civilians were killed by army
- Somber reminder of Guatemala's 36-year Civil War (1960-1996)
- Santiago was scene of significant conflict during this period

**Activities & Attractions**

*Nature & Wildlife*
- **Atitlán Nature Birding/Quetzal Tours**: Rey Tepepul Nature Reserve
  - Early morning hikes (4:30-5:30 AM start) to see resplendent quetzal in wild
  - Guatemala's national bird, considered sacred by Maya
  - Cloud forest habitat with monkeys, rare birds, potentially snakes
  - Approximately 3-hour hike to waterfall
  - Local guides essential for wildlife spotting

*Volcano Hiking*
- **Volcán San Pedro**: Most popular of the three lake volcanoes
  - 3-5 hours to summit from San Pedro La Laguna (shorter route)
  - 4-7 hours from Santiago Atitlán
  - Spectacular views of lake and Pacific Ocean on clear days
  - Can be done as day hike or overnight camping trip

- **Volcán Tolimán**: Intermediate difficulty
  - Two peaks (north and south), one with imploded crater
  - 3-5 hours to summit
  - Cloud forest trail
  - Overnight camping possible

- **Volcán Atitlán**: Most challenging, least hiked
  - 5-8 hours to summit through coffee plantations and maize fields
  - Best as overnight experience for sunset/sunrise
  - Requires experienced local guide

*Cultural Experiences*
- Market days: Traditional Maya market with textiles, produce, crafts
- Art galleries: Local Tz'utujil Maya art and traditional paintings
- Pottery and ceramic workshops
- Traditional fishing demonstrations on the lake
- Kayaking tours exploring the lake
- Shaman and Sacred Caves Tour: Fire cleansing ritual, Mayan calendar teachings

*Day Trip Villages*
- **San Juan La Laguna**: Art galleries, textile cooperatives, colorful murals
- **San Pedro La Laguna**: Backpacker hub with hiking, restaurants
- **San Marcos La Laguna**: Wellness/yoga/meditation center
- **Santa Catarina Palopó**: Thermal waters, volcano viewpoints
- Boat (lancha) transport between villages readily available

**Practicalities**
- Restaurants along waterfront serving traditional Guatemalan cuisine
- Los Elementos Day Spa for post-ride massage
- Accommodations range from budget hostels to boutique lakefront hotels
- Banks and ATMs available in larger towns
- Boat schedules run throughout daylight hours between villages

### Riding Notes

- Another big climbing day: 2,250m gain
- BUT also 3,022m of descent - net loss of 772m
- You'll climb high into mountains, then descend to lake
- Spectacular scenery throughout
- Arrive at lake level (1,568m) for Santiago Atitlán
- Minimal unpaved sections (< 1%)
- Consider timing: earlier start means more daylight at the lake
- November weather should be ideal - dry and clear

## Stage 109: Santiago Atitlán to Antigua (79 km)
**Thursday, November 27, 2025 - Thanksgiving Day**

### To Colonial Glory

Your final stage of this section brings you from the mystical lake to Guatemala's crown jewel: Antigua, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most beautiful colonial cities in the Americas. This Thanksgiving Day ride takes you through Guatemala's highlands on a route with more climbing (1,763m) but rewarding you with arrival in one of Central America's most spectacular destinations.

### Along the Route

The route from Santiago Atitlán to Antigua takes you away from the lake, climbing through the Guatemalan highlands and passing through Maya villages and coffee country. You'll likely pass through or near:

**Panajachel**
- Main tourist hub of Lake Atitlán (if route passes through)
- Santander Street (Calle Santander): Tourist markets, restaurants
- Departure point for boat tours

**Highland Villages**
- Traditional K'iche' and Kaqchikel Maya communities
- Colorful traditional dress and textiles
- Agricultural lands: coffee, corn, vegetables
- Views of volcanic peaks throughout

### Arrival: Antigua Guatemala

**UNESCO World Heritage Status (1979)**

Antigua is a perfectly preserved Spanish colonial city, designated for its outstanding Baroque architecture, grid-pattern urban planning, and historical significance. It served as the capital of the Spanish Kingdom of Guatemala (which included most of Central America) for over 230 years until devastating earthquakes in 1773 forced the capital's relocation to present-day Guatemala City.

**Historical Context**

*Founding & Name*
- Founded March 10, 1543 as "Santiago de los Caballeros de Guatemala"
- Third location for the colonial capital (previous cities destroyed by indigenous uprising and volcanic mudflow)
- Located in the Valley of Panchoy at 1,530m elevation
- Renamed "La Antigua Guatemala" ("Old Guatemala") after 1776 capital relocation
- Grid pattern inspired by Italian Renaissance urban planning principles

*Colonial Importance*
- Seat of the Captaincy-General of Guatemala (1543-1773)
- Covered territory: Guatemala, Belize, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, and Chiapas (Mexico)
- Cultural, economic, religious, political, and educational center for entire region
- Home to universities, printing presses, churches, monasteries

*Earthquake History*
- Built in earthquake-prone region surrounded by volcanoes
- Suffered numerous destructive earthquakes throughout colonial period
- 1773 Santa Marta earthquakes caused catastrophic damage
- Many buildings preserved as magnificent ruins
- Partial abandonment allowed preservation of colonial character

**Major Attractions in Antigua**

*Iconic Landmarks*
- **Arco de Santa Catalina**: Yellow arch spanning the street, most photographed landmark
  - Built 1693, frames views of Volcán de Agua
  - Originally connected Santa Catalina convent to school across street
  - Perfect photo spot, especially early morning

*Churches & Religious Sites*
- **La Merced Church**: Spectacular Baroque yellow facade with intricate details
  - Large fountain in convent courtyard
  - Active church with beautiful interior
  
- **Cathedral of San José** (Cathedral Ruins): Main plaza landmark
  - Partially destroyed by earthquakes, mixture of active church and ruins
  - Holds significant religious and historical importance

- **San Francisco Church & Monastery**: Beautiful colonial complex

- **Iglesia de La Recolección**: Impressive ruins of former monastery

- **Convento de Capuchinas**: Well-preserved convent ruins
  - Unique circular architecture

*Museums & Cultural Centers*
- **Museo Casa Santo Domingo**: Former monastery, now hotel and museum complex
  - Extensive colonial art, archaeology, Mayan artifacts
  - Beautiful gardens and courtyards

- **Palace of the Captains General**: Historic government building on plaza
  - Now houses government offices and cultural spaces

- **ChocoMuseo**: Interactive chocolate-making workshops
  - Learn bean-to-bar process
  - Family-friendly activity

*Parks & Viewpoints*
- **Parque Central** (Central Park/Plaza Mayor): Heart of the city
  - Reconstructed fountain
  - Surrounded by cathedral, palace, municipal buildings
  - Popular gathering spot, cafes and restaurants nearby

- **Cerro de la Cruz**: Viewpoint overlooking city and Volcán de Agua
  - 15-20 minute walk uphill from center
  - Panoramic views, best at sunset
  - Go with group or guide for safety

**Volcanic Setting**

*Volcán de Agua* (3,760m)
- Dominates Antigua's southern skyline
- Perfect cone shape
- Responsible for 1541 disaster that destroyed second colonial capital
- Can be climbed with guide

*Volcán de Fuego* (3,763m - Active!)
- Visible from Antigua, frequently active
- Regular eruptions with lava and ash
- Not climbable due to danger

*Volcán Acatenango* (3,976m)
- Most popular volcano hike from Antigua
- Overnight camping trips to watch Fuego erupt from safe vantage point
- Challenging but spectacular experience

**Activities & Tours**

*Coffee Plantation Tours*
- **Finca Filadelfia**: Learn bean-to-cup process, tastings with different methods
- Several other fincas offer tours
- Antigua region produces premium Guatemalan coffee

*Cooking Classes*
- Learn traditional Guatemalan dishes: pepián, jocón, tamales
- Several cooking schools offer classes with market tours

*Market Shopping*
- **Mercado de Artesanías**: Handicrafts, textiles, jewelry
- Excellent place for souvenirs
- Traditional Maya weavings, jade, ceramics

*Semana Santa (Holy Week)*
- Antigua is world-famous for Holy Week processions (March/April)
- Elaborate sawdust carpets (alfombras) along procession routes
- You won't see this in November, but the tradition is deeply important here

**Food & Dining**

Must-try restaurants:
- **Rincon Tipico**: Authentic, affordable Guatemalan food (try pepián, grilled chicken)
- **Café Sky**: Famous rooftop views, international and Guatemalan cuisine, best at sunset
- **El Tenedor del Cerro**: Mountain views, elevated dining
- **Hector's Bistro**: Mexican food, tacos, margaritas, artsy atmosphere
- Numerous cafes serving excellent Guatemalan coffee

Traditional dishes to try:
- Pepián: Rich meat stew with roasted vegetables and spices
- Jocón: Chicken in green sauce
- Tamales: Corn masa with fillings, wrapped in banana leaves
- Rellenitos: Sweet plantain filled with sweetened black beans
- Atol: Traditional warm corn-based beverage

**Practical Information**

*Getting Around*
- Very walkable city - most attractions within 20 minutes on foot
- Cobblestone streets (watch your footing)
- Tuk-tuks available for longer distances or tired legs
- Uber operates in Antigua

*Climate in November*
- Perfect timing! Dry season, clear skies
- Daytime: 20-24°C (68-75°F)
- Nights: 10-14°C (50-57°F)
- Bring light layers
- Elevation 1,530m means mild temperatures year-round

*Safety Notes*
- Generally safe tourist city with visible police presence
- Use normal urban precautions
- Avoid walking to Cerro de la Cruz alone - go with group or tour
- Keep valuables secure in crowded areas

*Rest Day Activities (Friday, November 28)*
- Explore colonial architecture and ruins
- Visit museums and chocolate workshops
- Coffee plantation tour
- Relax in cafes and restaurants
- Shop for textiles and crafts
- Volcano hike planning for Acatenango (if interested)
- Simply wander the beautiful cobblestone streets

## Regional Context: Mexico to Guatemala Transition

### Coffee Heritage

Both sides of this border are world-renowned coffee regions. The route takes you through the heart of Central America's coffee belt:

**Mexican Coffee (Chiapas)**
- German immigrants established plantations in late 1800s
- High-altitude Arabica beans
- Shade-grown, often organic
- Soconusco region (around Tapachula) produces premium beans
- Many original German family plantations still operating

**Guatemalan Coffee**
- Guatemala ranks among world's top 10 coffee producers
- Eight distinct coffee-growing regions
- Antigua coffee is particularly prized (you'll taste it there!)
- Volcanic soil provides ideal growing conditions
- Elevation creates perfect temperature ranges

### Maya Cultural Continuity

You're riding through one of the heartlands of Maya civilization:

**Ancient Maya**
- Izapa (1500 BCE - 1200 CE): Bridge between Olmec and Maya
- Kaminaljuyú (near modern Guatemala City): Major highland center
- Highland Guatemala was home to powerful K'iche' and Kaqchikel kingdoms

**Modern Maya Culture**
- Guatemala is over 40% indigenous Maya (higher than any Central American country)
- Traditional dress (traje) still worn daily, especially in highlands
- Maya languages actively spoken (K'iche', Kaqchikel, Tz'utujil, Mam, others)
- Traditional crafts, textiles, spiritual practices continue
- Markets remain centers of community life

**Historical Trauma**
- Spanish conquest (1524) violently disrupted Maya civilizations
- Guatemalan Civil War (1960-1996) disproportionately affected Maya communities
- Estimated 200,000 killed, mostly Maya civilians
- Truth and Reconciliation efforts ongoing
- Santiago Atitlán massacre memorial is one of many across Guatemala

### Volcanic Ring of Fire

This entire region sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire:

**Major Volcanoes You'll See**
- **Tacaná** (4,060m): Mexico-Guatemala border, Mexico's sixth-highest peak
- **Santa María** (3,770m): Perfect cone above Quetzaltenango
- **Santiaguito** (2,500m): Active dome, frequent small eruptions
- **Atitlán** (3,535m): Towers over Lake Atitlán
- **Tolimán** (3,158m): Lake Atitlán's second guardian
- **San Pedro** (3,020m): Popular hiking volcano
- **Acatenango** (3,976m): Near Antigua, camping destination
- **Fuego** (3,763m): Very active, visible eruptions from Antigua
- **Agua** (3,760m): Iconic backdrop to Antigua

**Geological Significance**
- Volcanic soil creates incredibly fertile agricultural land
- Hot springs and geothermal activity throughout region
- Earthquakes remain constant threat
- Volcanic ash enriches coffee-growing conditions
- Crater lakes like Atitlán formed by ancient eruptions

### Border Crossing Practicalities

**Mexico Exit**
- Ensure all paperwork complete
- Temporary import permits (TIP) for bicycle should be cancelled
- Get exit stamp in passport

**Guatemala Entry**
- Passport required (valid 6 months beyond travel dates)
- US, Canadian, EU citizens: 90-day tourist visa granted at border (free)
- CA-4 Agreement: Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua share 90-day limit
- No bicycle import fees typically required for tourism
- May be asked about onward travel plans

**Currency**
- Mexico: Mexican Peso (MXN)
- Guatemala: Quetzal (GTQ)
- Exchange money at border or in Tapachula before crossing
- ATMs widely available in Guatemalan cities
- US dollars sometimes accepted at tourist areas
- Credit cards accepted at most hotels and restaurants

**Language**
- Spanish is official language in both countries
- Guatemala has 21 officially recognized Maya languages
- In tourist areas (Antigua, Lake Atitlán), English widely spoken
- Rural areas: Spanish essential, some areas primarily Maya languages

**Time Zone**
- Both Mexico (Chiapas) and Guatemala use CST (UTC-6)
- No time change at border
- Guatemala does not observe daylight saving time

### Altitude Considerations

You'll experience significant elevation changes:

**Elevation Profile**
- Tapachula: 116m (sea level)
- Coatepeque: 465m (low hills)
- Quetzaltenango: 2,340m (HIGH - take it easy!)
- Santiago Atitlán: 1,568m (moderate)
- Antigua: 1,532m (moderate)

**Altitude Tips**
- Stay hydrated
- Eat carbohydrates for energy
- Quetzaltenango is high enough that some people feel effects
- Sleep lower if possible (Coatepeque, Santiago, Antigua all lower than Xela)
- Headache, fatigue, shortness of breath = mild altitude effects
- Symptoms usually improve after 24 hours at altitude
- Descent to Lake Atitlán provides relief

### November Weather Patterns

**Dry Season Arrives**
- November marks beginning of dry season (November-April)
- Expect sunny, clear days
- Cool mornings, warm afternoons
- Cold nights at high elevations (Quetzaltenango especially)
- Low humidity compared to rainy season
- Ideal conditions for cycling and photography
- Clear volcano views most days

**Temperature Ranges by Location**
- Tapachula (coastal): Hot, 25-35°C (77-95°F)
- Quetzaltenango: Cold nights, cool days, 5-20°C (40-68°F)
- Lake Atitlán: Mild, 15-25°C (59-77°F)
- Antigua: Perfect, 15-24°C (59-75°F)

### Health & Safety

**Vaccinations**
- Routine vaccines up to date
- Hepatitis A recommended
- Typhoid recommended for rural areas
- Yellow Fever not required
- Rabies if planning animal contact

**Water**
- Don't drink tap water
- Bottled water widely available
- Purification tablets as backup
- Fruits and vegetables: wash or peel

**Mosquitoes**
- Dengue fever present (especially coastal areas)
- Zika virus present (low risk, coastal areas)
- Malaria very low risk in Guatemala highlands
- Use repellent, especially dawn/dusk
- Long sleeves/pants in evening

**Food Safety**
- Stick to cooked foods when possible
- Street food generally safe if well-cooked
- Fruits you can peel are safest
- Avoid raw vegetables unless washed with purified water

**Road Safety**
- Traffic can be chaotic
- Watch for buses (chicken buses especially)
- Dogs may chase cyclists
- Cobblestones slippery when wet
- Highland roads may have fog

8. **Coffee plantations** surrounding routes
9. **Quetzaltenango** historic buildings and plaza
10. **Border crossing** - document your entry to Guatemala

### Photography Tips

- **Golden hour**: Sunrise and sunset provide best light
- **Volcano views**: Mornings typically clearer before afternoon clouds
- **Markets**: Ask permission before photographing people
- **Churches**: Check if photography allowed inside
- **Textiles**: Bright colors pop in direct sunlight
- **Bring extra batteries**: Cold highland nights drain batteries

## Final Thoughts

These four stages represent one of the most culturally and scenically diverse sections of the North American Epic. You'll transition from Mexico to Central America, climb from near sea level to over 2,300 meters, experience the profound beauty of Lake Atitlán, and arrive in one of the Americas' most magnificent colonial cities.

The climbing is significant (especially Stage 107), but the rewards are extraordinary. Each destination offers something unique:
- **Tapachula**: Coffee heritage and Olmec-Maya archaeology  
- **Guatemala**: Border crossing into new country
- **Quetzaltenango**: Authentic Maya highland city
- **Lake Atitlán**: Natural wonder with indigenous communities
- **Antigua**: Colonial architectural masterpiece

Take time on your rest days to fully experience these places. The coffee, the culture, the volcán views, the Maya traditions - this is what makes long-distance touring through Central America unforgettable.

Enjoy the ride, stay safe, and soak in every moment of this spectacular section!
